LEARNING CENTRE

KNOWLEDGE IS POWER.

Short, practical lessons about habits, attention, motivation and wellbeing. Educational information is not a substitute for professional support.

01 · How habits work

Habits often follow a cue, a behaviour and a reward. Noticing the cue gives you a chance to choose a different response.

READ THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Track the situation around a habit: time, place, mood and what happened just before. Change one part of the routine, such as moving to another room or keeping your phone away during a specific task.

02 · Motivation comes and goes

Motivation can help you begin, but routines and realistic plans make it easier to continue.

READ THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Make the first step small enough to start. Instead of “study all evening”, try “open the book and work for ten minutes”.

03 · Protecting attention

Notifications, endless feeds and constant switching can make focused work harder.

READ THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Silence non-essential notifications, keep only the task you need open and take planned breaks. You can also charge your phone away from your study space.

04 · Stress and emotions

Stress, boredom and loneliness can influence decisions. Emotions are information, not commands.

READ THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Name what you are feeling, pause, and choose a response that supports what matters to you. Reach out to a trusted person when things feel difficult.

05 · A setback is information

One difficult day does not erase the effort you have made.

READ THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Ask: What was happening? What did I need? What could I adjust next time? Avoid turning a moment into a judgement about yourself.

06 · When to get support

If a habit, mood or worry is affecting daily life, talking to a trusted adult or qualified professional can help.

READ THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

You can speak with a school counsellor, healthcare professional or trusted family member. In South Africa, Childline is available on 116 for children and young people needing support.

Learning references: habit-loop concepts are commonly discussed in behavioural psychology; attention and notification guidance is consistent with digital-wellbeing research. RESET lessons are educational summaries, not clinical advice. For health concerns, speak to a qualified professional.
